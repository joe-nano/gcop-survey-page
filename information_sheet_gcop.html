Participant information sheet: gCOP audit survey

Version 0.1, 8 November 2026. Please save or print this page to keep.

Audit title

Full title: Global Patient co-Owned Cloud (GPOC): a re-audit of health ministries and organisations, and a gCOP audit survey of COP31 participants

Simple title: Who should own health records, and what should they cost the climate? The gCOP audit survey. A separate information sheet covers the GPOC re-audit (the 2022 questions asked again), which you may also choose to answer.

Audit lead: Dr Joseph Davids, Institute of Global Health Innovation, Imperial College London. Senior audit lead: Prof Hutan Ashrafian, Institute of Global Health Innovation, Imperial College London. Audit team: Mohamed ElSharkawy and Kathrina Crystallis (Institute of Global Health Innovation, Imperial College London); Ramesh Nadarajah, Syed Aftab, Fady Sedra and Norman Kufwakwaro (Complex Spine London); Hadi Mohammed Abdullah (Korle Bu Teaching Hospital, Ghana). Only the audit lead and the senior audit lead handle your answers; the rest of the audit team see only group results.

Invitation paragraph

We invite you to answer a short online survey. Before you decide, please read this sheet: it explains why we are doing the survey and what it involves. Take your time, and talk to others if you wish.

Ask us if there is anything that is not clear or if you would like more information. Take time to decide whether or not you wish to take part.

Thank you for reading this.

Do I have to take part?

No. It is your choice. Your delegation or organisation will not be told whether you took part. Nothing is saved until you press Submit.

If you decide to take part, please keep this sheet (save or print it). You give consent by ticking boxes in the first section of the online form; there is nothing to sign. You are still free to withdraw at any time and without giving a reason. This is what happens to your answers if you withdraw:

  • Before you submit: close the page. Nothing is kept.
  • After you submit: open the short withdrawal form on the survey page and enter your code. You do not need to contact anyone. We delete your answers. If the withdrawal form is not working, email your code to jdavids@ic.ac.uk with 'Withdraw' in the subject. This tells us your email address, but we delete your answers without reading them, keep only the code and date in our deletion log, and delete your email.
  • Deadline: the final results are made on 4 December 2026. Withdrawals received by 23:59 UK time on 4 December are removed from all final results.
  • Without a code we cannot find your answers, so we cannot delete them.
  • Group totals already published cannot be recalled, but they hold no personal information.

What is the purpose of this audit?

In 2022, 267 people from health ministries and international organisations told us whether patients should co-own their medical records in a shared, non-commercial and secure online store.

The gCOP survey asks people at the UN climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye (COP31) how health records, the energy that digital health uses, and preparing health systems for climate change should fit into the climate talks. We compare the answers with the readiness levels set in our earlier GPOC work, which is why we call it an audit. The questions may be refined after expert review before the survey opens; any revised version is issued as a new version before it is used.

This is not a student project.

This is an audit under Imperial College London guidance, so it does not need ethical review.

Why have I been invited?

You are registered for COP31. We are inviting people from every country, every ministry and every observer group, by email to institutional addresses and through focal points who forward the invitation. There is no fixed number; in earlier surveys of COP participants, between 5 and 20 in every 100 people invited took part.

What will happen to me if I take part?

Everything happens online, on your own phone or computer. There are no visits, tests, audio recordings, photographs or videos. You will:

  • Note the code shown on the survey page, then open the survey. It is a Microsoft Form run by Imperial College London.
  • Enter your code, tick a few boxes to agree, and make three optional choices.
  • Answer five questions about your role, then 42 statements from Strongly agree to Strongly disagree. You can skip any.
  • Answer three opinion questions: each time, say which of two ways to set up a health-records service you think is better. This takes about 12 minutes in total.
  • If you wish, answer the 2022 questions as well, in a second form (the GPOC re-audit, with its own information sheet): about 10 more minutes.

These times are estimates; we will check them with 10-15 people before the survey opens.

Your anonymous code. The survey page shows a random code that looks like GP-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-C. Your browser makes it on the survey page. It is not made from your name, email, badge or any other personal detail. You type it into each form (in some links it may already be filled in). Keep a note of it: you need it to link your answers to the two surveys and to withdraw. You can take part without it, but then we cannot link or remove your answers.

Where your answers go. The form does not record your name or email. Your answers are kept without your name in Imperial College London's Microsoft 365 account. After the analysis, a copy with nothing that identifies you is kept on Imperial's secure data store (RDS) until 31 December 2039 (permission to hold this data classification on the RDS to be confirmed by Imperial's Data Protection Office before any data are collected; if not permitted, long-term storage stays in Imperial Microsoft 365). See "How will we use information about you?" below.

If we invited you by email. Your published work address is in a separate invitation list. We use it only for the emails in our recruitment plan. Ministries, organisations and focal points get one invitation and at most 3 reminders. Leaders invited by name get one invitation and at most one follow-up. Everyone invited gets one email with the results. The list is never linked to anyone's answers. We delete it by 31 January 2027.

Thank you. Participants are not paid or given a gift. Imperial's gift and anti-bribery rules make payment inappropriate for officials and delegates. Everyone can read the summary of results.

What do I have to do?

Nothing beyond the steps above. There are no restrictions on what you eat or drink, driving, medicines, sport or anything else.

What are the possible disadvantages and risks of taking part?

Some questions ask about your country or organisation. Please answer as yourself. We never treat answers as a government's position, and we never show results for any group of fewer than 10 people.

We do not ask your name, but your country and role together might point to you in a small delegation. So we protect your answers as personal information.

What are the possible benefits of taking part?

There is no direct benefit to you. We cannot promise the audit will help you, but the results may help make health records safer, fairer and lower in carbon for patients everywhere.

What will happen to the results of this audit?

We will publish group results in an audit report and lay summaries on the survey page (the landing page linked from your invitation). No one will be named, and no group of fewer than 10 people is shown.

We do not hold your contact details with your answers, so we cannot send you your own results. If we invited you by email, we send one email with the group results to that address. The results will be on the survey page.

Microsoft 365 data location and IP logging: to be confirmed by Imperial ICT before any data are collected.

Imperial College London is responsible for looking after your information. We will keep this information about you safe and secure by:

  • A data management plan covers the collection, movement, processing and storage of the data.
  • Answers are collected on Microsoft Forms in Imperial's Microsoft 365 account and kept in the audit lead's Imperial OneDrive or SharePoint. The de-identified dataset is then kept on Imperial's secure data store (RDS) until 31 December 2039 (permission to hold this data classification on the RDS to be confirmed by Imperial's Data Protection Office before any data are collected; if not permitted, long-term storage stays in Imperial Microsoft 365).

  • Only totals for groups of 10 or more people are published. No answers go on any blockchain or into the GPOC software during this audit.

Once we have finished the audit, we will keep some of the data so we can check the results. We will write our reports in a way that no-one can work out that you took part in the audit.
